# 23. Open Items & Clarifications

> **What this section is.** A structured backlog of architectural concerns identified after the main SDD was authored, by a reviewer running with cleared context. Each item comes with a **Recommended Answer** - a concrete, ready-to-apply resolution. Items are decisions awaiting the architect's acceptance: accept the recommendation (or adjust it), and it gets reflected into the SDD body.
>
> **What this section is not.** It is not a list of inline `[NEEDS CLARIFICATION: ...]` markers found in the body - those remain inline. This section is the reviewer's *external* findings: gaps the body did not mark, scenarios the body did not consider, corner cases the body did not test for, and contract inconsistencies between the centralized catalogues (chunks 10, 11, 12) and the per-service chunks they consolidate.

## How to read each item

| Field | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **ID** | OI-NN. Stable across revisions. |

| **Type** | Architecture gap / Missing scenario / Corner case / Ambiguity / Risk / Inconsistency / NFR shortfall / ADR needed / Contract mismatch (topic, event, payload, consumer list, or role/permission divergence across chunks) / Duplication (BRD content or another chunk's content restated instead of referenced). |

### OI-01: Two module dependency cycles fail the build's boundary check

- **Where:** §10 ADR-01 (How), §9 AP-11, §15.2 and §15.3 (API-12, API-14), §17.1 Developer Notes, §14.10 (`RefundRequestApproved`, `PayoutSucceeded`, `PayoutFailedFinally`)
- **Type:** Architecture gap
- **Concern:** ADR-01 and AP-11 verify the module boundaries on every build with Spring Modulith (§6 Backend Runtime), and that verification requires the dependencies between modules to form a directed acyclic graph. Two pairs of modules depend on each other. customer-accounts calls notifications through API-14 (`MessageDispatchPort.sendNow`, provider notifications in §15.2) while notifications calls customer-accounts through API-12 (`CustomerContactPort.getContact`). refund-requests handles `PayoutSucceeded` and `PayoutFailedFinally`, published by payouts, while payouts handles `RefundRequestApproved`, published by refund-requests, and a listener's parameter type is a type dependency on the module that declares it. Ownership of `MessageDispatchPort` is also unclear: §15.2 makes it the provider's port (notifications), while §17.1 Developer Notes lists it among customer-accounts' own hexagonal ports. As written, the first build fails the stated check, or the team weakens it, and the LLD inherits a package layout no chunk states. Whether listener-only type references count toward the cycle check should be confirmed on the first build; the resolution below removes both cycles either way.
- **Options:**
  - **A.** Point every dependency one way: customer-accounts owns the API-14 interface as its outbound port and notifications implements it, and payouts declares the three payout contract DTOs in its published API package - no new module and no event renamed; one interface and three DTO records change package.
  - **B.** Move the shared contract types into a shared kernel module that every module may use - one place for contracts; a shared module erodes the bounded contexts of the §6 DDD rule and tends to grow.
  - **C.** Relax or skip the cycle rule - no change now; AP-11 loses the check it relies on, and the first extraction (ADR-01 trigger) must cut two two-way couplings.
- **Recommended Answer:** Option A - three edits:
  - §15.2, API-14 row, Method & URI: "`MessageDispatchPort.sendNow` (outbound port owned by customer-accounts, implemented by an adapter in notifications)"; §15.3 API-14, Port interface: "`MessageDispatchPort` (owned by customer-accounts; notifications implements it)".
  - §14.10, add above the table: "**Contract ownership:** the DTO records of `RefundRequestApproved`, `PayoutSucceeded`, and `PayoutFailedFinally` are declared in the published API package of payouts; refund-requests publishes the first and handles the other two, so refund-requests depends on payouts and payouts on no other module. Every other DTO is declared by its publisher."
  - §9 AP-11, append: "The module graph is acyclic: notifications depends on customer-accounts and refund-requests; customer-accounts and loyalty-points depend on refund-requests; refund-requests depends on payouts; payouts depends on no module."
- **Why:** AP-11 and ADR-01 make the boundary check a build gate, so a cycle is a defect, not a matter of style; option A is the port rule the SDD already states (§6: modules call each other only through ports, and adapters plug into ports) applied in one direction, and it keeps payouts a reusable lower module, as the outcome re-publication doctrine of §14.7 implies. The tradeoff accepted: refund-requests publishes a type that payouts declares, which changes no name in the §14.10 registry.
- **Status:** Accepted - applied (§9 AP-11, §14.10, §15.2, §15.3 API-14, §17.1, §17.4)

### OI-02: ADR-01 reads both BRDs as silent on teams, but LOYALTY names an owner for the Refunds Portal

- **Where:** §10 ADR-01 (Why), §8.1.2 (Stage and team), §3
- **Type:** Ambiguity
- **Concern:** ADR-01 rests its first driver on "one team assumed (both BRDs silent)", and its extraction trigger fires "when a second team takes over one product's modules". LOYALTY is not silent: [LOYALTY 02 § Dependencies](../brd-loyalty-points/02-glossary-assumptions-facts.md#dependencies) names the Finance team as owner of the Refunds Portal, the dependency that provides refund outcomes, while REFUNDS names no owning team for its product. If that owner is a Finance delivery team, the trigger is met on day one and refund-requests and payouts belong in their own deployable, with `RefundPaid` as an integration event; if it is the business owner of refunds, ADR-01 stands but quotes its evidence wrongly. The BRDs do not say which reading holds; only the business can.
- **Options:**
  - **A.** Cite LOYALTY 02 in ADR-01, record the reading (the Finance team is the business owner; one delivery team builds both products) as a technical assumption to confirm, and state what changes if it is wrong - the decision stands on accurate evidence; one question to the approver of both BRDs.
  - **B.** Treat the Finance team as a second delivery team now and choose the hybrid style - right if confirmed; a broker and a second deployable before any evidence requires them.
- **Recommended Answer:** Option A - three edits:
  - §10 ADR-01, Why: "one team assumed (both BRDs silent)" becomes "one delivery team assumed for both products; [LOYALTY 02 § Dependencies](../brd-loyalty-points/02-glossary-assumptions-facts.md#dependencies) names the Finance team as owner of the Refunds Portal, read here as its business owner (§3 Assumption 7)".
  - §3, append: "7. **One delivery team:** one delivery team builds and runs both products; the Finance team owns the Refunds Portal as its business owner ([LOYALTY 02 § Dependencies](../brd-loyalty-points/02-glossary-assumptions-facts.md#dependencies)). [NEEDS CLARIFICATION: confirm with the Head of Retail that the Finance team has no delivery team of its own for the Refunds Portal.] If it has, the ADR-01 extraction trigger is met at the start."
  - §8.1.2, Stage and team: "built by one assumed team (questionnaire Q1, Q2; ...)" becomes "built by one delivery team (§3 Assumption 7; questionnaire Q1, Q2; ...)", with the links unchanged.
- **Why:** the team count is the first driver of ADR-01 and the only one tied to its extraction trigger, so its evidence must match the BRDs; option A costs one question to the Head of Retail, who approved version 1.0 of both BRDs, and avoids building for a second team the business may not have.
- **Status:** Accepted - applied (§3 Assumption 7, §8.1.2, §10 ADR-01)

### OI-03: The outbox rule is stated as absolute, but four synchronous outside calls are designed

- **Where:** §1 (paragraph 2), §8.1.1, §9 AP-03, §10 ADR-02 (Decision), §14.7, §15.5
- **Type:** Inconsistency
- **Concern:** §8.1.1 says "Every call that leaves the process (Payment Provider, Notification Partner, POS Records) runs from the publication log after the business transaction commits", AP-03 says a module calls a provider "only from the publication log after commit", and §1 and ADR-02 call the log "the outbox for every call that leaves the process". The design has four synchronous outside calls that never pass through the log: the receipt lookup (API-01, §17.2), the code sends of API-14 through API-05 (§8.5.3, ADR-05), the branch assignment reads (API-06, §17.2), and the Keycloak admin calls of customer-accounts (§17.1). Only §14.7 words the rule as intended ("every call to an outside system that follows a state change"). §15.5 also counts API-14 followed by API-05 as one hop, although it is a module port call followed by a provider call inside one user request. An implementer who follows AP-03 literally routes the code sends through the log and loses the immediate answer of [REFUNDS/UC-06](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-06-sign-up-and-sign-in) E3.
- **Options:**
  - **A.** Reword the absolute statements to the §14.7 rule and list the allowed synchronous outside calls once, each made with no database transaction open - one rule and one list; five places to edit.
  - **B.** Route every outside call through the log, the lookups and the codes included - one uniform rule; breaks [REFUNDS/UC-01](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-01-request-a-refund) E4 and [REFUNDS/UC-06](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-06-sign-up-and-sign-in) E3, which need an answer while the customer waits.
- **Recommended Answer:** Option A - four edits:
  - §8.1.1, sentence 3 becomes: "Every call to an outside system that follows a committed state change runs from the publication log after commit, never inside the business transaction. Four synchronous outside calls are allowed, each made with no database transaction open and with its §12 timeout: the receipt lookup (API-01), the code sends of API-14 through API-05 (ADR-05), the branch assignment reads (API-06), and the Keycloak admin calls of the sign-up, confirmation, and password reset requests (§17.1)."
  - §9 AP-03, Description becomes: "Modules react to durable in-process domain events (§14.10); an outside call that follows a committed state change runs only from the publication log after commit, never inside the business transaction; the synchronous exceptions are listed in §8.1.1 (ADR-02, ADR-05)."
  - §1 paragraph 2 and §10 ADR-02 (Decision): "every call that leaves the process" becomes "every outside call that follows a committed state change (exceptions in §8.1.1)".
  - §15.5, append: "API-14 followed by its API-05 send is the one accepted two-step synchronous path inside a user request (a module port, then the provider); its time budget is in §12 INT-02."
- **Why:** the synchronous calls are deliberate (ADR-05, [REFUNDS/UC-01](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-01-request-a-refund) E4, and [REFUNDS/UC-06](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-06-sign-up-and-sign-in) E3 need them), so the doctrine text, not the design, is wrong; one list stops each chunk from carrying its own version of the rule. The tradeoff accepted: a doctrine with named exceptions instead of an absolute one.
- **Status:** Accepted - applied (§1, §8.1.1, §9 AP-03, §10 ADR-02, §15.5)

### OI-05: The publication log cannot deliver the retry behaviour the integrations promise

- **Where:** §11.1 (Publication log), §11.4 (Alerting), §10 ADR-02, §12 INT-02 and INT-03, §15.3 API-02 and API-05, §17.4 (Delivery, Figure 25), §9 AP-10
- **Type:** Inconsistency
- **Concern:** §11.1 resubmits incomplete entries "on restart and on a schedule", while §12 INT-02 and INT-03, §15.3 API-02 and API-05, and §17.4 retry event-driven provider calls from the publication log with backoff, INT-02 "with exponential backoff and jitter". The log keeps no attempt count or next-try time, so a scheduled resubmission retries every waiting entry at the same cadence: no backoff, no jitter, and every waiting call at once when a partner recovers. With at least 2 replicas (§11.3), a replica that restarts during a rolling update resubmits entries the other replica is still running, so one listener can run twice at the same moment; the notifications check "Message already sent for this key?" (Figure 25) is a check-then-act that two concurrent runs can both pass, sending a message twice against the rule of §17.4. The §11.4 alert on entries incomplete after 15 minutes fires for every message waiting out a partner outage, so it cannot tell a stuck listener from a planned retry. A release that renames, moves, or removes a listener leaves its incomplete entries with no target; AP-10 covers DTO changes only. The give-up limits and poison-message strategies marked inline in §12 and in each 13x chunk stay there; this item covers the shared mechanism.
- **Options:**
  - **A.** Keep the log as the durable trigger only: a listener that leads to an outside call records the work in its own schema under a unique key and completes, and a send job of its module makes the calls with backoff and jitter; one job resubmits stale entries; listener identities stay stable across releases - matches §17.3, which already works this way; a work record and a send job in notifications and in the POS adapter of refund-requests.
  - **B.** Keep the calls in the listeners, add an attempt record per listener that skips the call until its next try is due, and resubmit stale entries every minute - fewer tables; each waiting entry is resubmitted every minute only to return early, and the 15-minute alert stays ambiguous.
  - **C.** Change §12 to "resubmitted on the §11.1 schedule" and drop backoff and jitter - one honest sentence; retry storms on recovery and concurrent runs remain.
- **Recommended Answer:** Option A - three edits:
  - §11.1, the Publication log bullet becomes: "**Publication log (modular monolith, durable in-process events, §14.10):** `platform.event_publication` (Spring Modulith registry), written in the publisher's transaction. A listener that leads to an outside call records the work in its own schema under a unique key made of the publication and the listener (the payout of §17.3, the message of §17.4, the items notice of §17.2) and completes; it may make the first try right after its commit, and a send job of its module (one replica, under the job lock) makes every later try with exponential backoff and jitter until success or the module's give-up limit. A single `publication-resubmit` job (one replica, under the job lock) resubmits entries incomplete for more than 5 minutes, once a minute; nothing is resubmitted on restart. Completed entries are deleted after 7 days. A release keeps listener identities stable; a release that renames, moves, or removes a listener ships a migration that completes or re-targets that listener's incomplete entries."
  - §12 INT-02 and INT-03, §15.3 API-05, and the §17.4 Delivery bullet: "retried from the publication log" becomes "retried by the module's send job (§11.1)"; §15.3 API-02: "Sent from the publication log after commit, retried" becomes "Recorded after commit and sent by the module's send job (§11.1), retried"; Figure 25 node I becomes "Record the message; the send job retries with backoff".
  - §11.4, Alerting: "incomplete publications older than 15 minutes" stays and now means a stuck listener; append "and every give-up of a send job".
- **Why:** REFUNDS/NFR-01 and the "never sends a message twice" rule of §17.4 need one run per effect, and the backoff and jitter of §12 exist to protect partners while they recover; option A reuses the shape §17.3 already has (a payout record plus the `payout-retry` job) and keeps the single durable mechanism of ADR-02. The tradeoff accepted: a work record and a send job in two more modules.
- **Status:** Accepted - applied (§11.1, §11.4, §12 INT-02 and INT-03, §15.3 API-02 and API-05, §17.4 and Figure 25)

### OI-06: Tenancy has no registry for jobs, no carrier for redelivered events, and no rule for platform tables

- **Where:** §11.2, §10 ADR-03 (Consequences), §9 AP-06, §14.1, §11.1, the scheduled jobs of §17.1 to §17.5
- **Type:** Architecture gap
- **Concern:** Row-level security filters every module table by a session setting the deployable sets per transaction, and the tenant comes from the token, the event, or the inbound credential (§11.2). Six scheduled jobs (`account-closure`, `waiting-requests-summary`, `branch-assignment-refresh`, `refund-record-retention`, `payout-retry`, `former-member-retention`) and the publication resubmission run with no request, and §17.3 says its retry job "runs per tenant", but nothing lists the tenants a job iterates, so a job sees no rows or needs a database role that bypasses row-level security. A resubmitted publication has no request context either; §14.1 says tenant and correlation context "travel with the publication" without saying how. ADR-03 and AP-06 put `tenant_id` first in every key and index of every table, yet `platform.event_publication` is a framework table without that column, and the Flyway history tables are the same. ADR-03's "a second retailer needs only provisioning" lists no steps, although the token claim mapper, the brokered identity providers, the provider credentials, and the branch data are all per tenant.
- **Options:**
  - **A.** A tenant registry in the `platform` schema that every job iterates, `tenantId` and `correlationId` in every in-process DTO, a named exemption for platform tables, and a provisioning list in ADR-03 - closes all four gaps; one registry to keep in step with Keycloak and Vault.
  - **B.** Give jobs a database role that bypasses row-level security and filter by `tenant_id` in code - no registry; one slip in a job reads or deletes across tenants, which §11.2 forbids.
- **Recommended Answer:** Option A - two edits:
  - §11.2, append three bullets: "**Tenant registry:** `platform.tenant` (`tenant_id`, name, status) lists the tenants; every scheduled job runs one transaction per active tenant with the tenant session setting set, and row-level security returns no row to a transaction without it." "**Tenant on redelivery:** every in-process event DTO carries `tenantId` and `correlationId`, and the listener sets both before it runs, so a resubmitted entry runs in its publisher's tenant without a request (§14.1)." "**Platform tables:** `platform.event_publication`, `platform.tenant`, any job-lock table, and the Flyway history tables are platform-owned and outside the `tenant_id`-first rule of ADR-03 and AP-06; no module query reads them."
  - §10 ADR-03, Consequences: "A second retailer needs only provisioning" becomes "A second retailer needs provisioning: a `platform.tenant` row, the Keycloak `tenant_id` mapper for its local accounts and for each identity provider it brokers, its provider credentials in Vault, its branch reference data, and its go-live date".
- **Why:** CLAUDE.md makes the multi-tenancy rules non-negotiable and requires tenant context on every outgoing call, which jobs and redeliveries cannot carry today without bypassing isolation; option A keeps row-level security as the last line of defence and turns "only provisioning" into a checklist. The tradeoff accepted: one platform table and two fields in every DTO.
- **Status:** Accepted - applied (§11.2, §10 ADR-03)

### OI-07: The gateway rejects token-less calls that public and provider routes must accept

- **Where:** §11.2 (Tenant context), §6 (API Gateway), §11.6, §15.1 (Standard headers), §8.3
- **Type:** Inconsistency
- **Concern:** §11.2 says "the gateway rejects a token without [the `tenant_id` claim]", and §6 says the gateway validates tokens. Figure 3 routes the provider callbacks and feeds (API-04, API-07, API-08, API-09) through the gateway, and §15.1 says those calls carry the provider's own signature or scheme, not a Keycloak token; the five sign-up and password reset endpoints of §17.1 are public. §15.1 Standard headers also marks `Authorization: Bearer` as required on every call. Read literally, the gateway drops every provider callback and every sign-up; read loosely, the provider paths are open to the internet with no stated control until an inbound adapter checks a scheme that is still `TBD - external`. The payout result (API-04) moves a request to Paid, so an open route is a money risk (REFUNDS/NFR-01).
- **Options:**
  - **A.** Three route classes at the gateway (user, public, provider), each with the checks that fit it - every path gets a stated control now; more gateway configuration.
  - **B.** A Keycloak client-credentials account with a `tenant_id` claim for each provider, so every route carries a token - one rule for all routes; works only if each provider can obtain OAuth2 tokens, which is `TBD - external` for all four.
- **Recommended Answer:** Option A - three edits:
  - §11.6, new bullet: "**Gateway route classes:** user routes need a Keycloak token with the `tenant_id` claim (§11.2). Public routes, the five sign-up and password reset endpoints of §17.1, need no token and are rate-limited per client address and per email address. Provider routes, the inbound endpoints of API-04, API-07, API-08, and API-09, need no Keycloak token; each accepts only its provider's source addresses, has a body-size limit and a rate limit per provider, and is authenticated by the provider's signature or mutual TLS in the inbound adapter (`TBD - external`), which maps the credential to the tenant (§11.2)."
  - §11.2, Tenant context: "the gateway rejects a token without it" becomes "on user routes, the gateway rejects a token without it".
  - §15.1, Standard headers, `Authorization` row, Required: "Yes on user routes; the provider's scheme on provider routes; none on public routes".
- **Why:** the provider schemes are unknown (§15.6), so the route policy must not wait for them; option A keeps token checks strict for users and adds the source-address limit that protects the payout callback until the provider's own check is known, while B remains the scheme for any provider that supports client credentials. The tradeoff accepted: three route classes to keep in the gateway configuration.
- **Status:** Accepted - applied (§11.2, §11.6, §15.1)

### OI-08: The Keycloak realm and client settings the design relies on are not stated

- **Where:** §10 ADR-07, §11.6, §16.8, §8.4.3
- **Type:** Architecture gap
- **Concern:** The design relies on Keycloak behaviour it never states. Sign-up must run only through customer-accounts so that every account has confirmed both addresses ([REFUNDS/UC-06](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-06-sign-up-and-sign-in) BR-1: confirm both addresses before the first request), and the password reset must use the code of [REFUNDS/UC-06](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-06-sign-up-and-sign-in) A3 (§8.4.3 sends the forgotten-password path from the Keycloak page to `POST /v1/password-resets`); Keycloak's own self-registration and reset-credentials features bypass both when they are on, and a self-registered user holding someone's email address also blocks that person's sign-up ([REFUNDS/UC-06](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-06-sign-up-and-sign-in) BR-2: one account per email address). One realm serves four populations through two web apps (ADR-07), but nothing says which sign-in options each app offers, so Loyalty Points web could offer the local customer sign-in and Refunds Portal web the member broker, mixing the identities R-12 keeps apart, and a realm session opened in one app can sign the same browser in to the other with the wrong identity. §16.8 ends a removed staff role "when its token expires" with no token lifetime, so the delay has no bound.
- **Options:**
  - **A.** State the realm and client settings in ADR-07: self-registration and built-in reset off, sign-in options per client, an identity check per app that forces a new sign-in, and token and session lifetimes - closes the bypasses and bounds revocation; realm configuration to keep under version control.
  - **B.** One realm per population - hard separation; breaks the CLAUDE.md single-realm rule that ADR-07 follows.
- **Recommended Answer:** Option A - two edits:
  - §10 ADR-07, How, append: "Realm self-registration and Keycloak's own reset-credentials flow are off: sign-up and password reset run only through customer-accounts (§17.1), and the sign-in page's forgotten-password link opens the Refunds Portal web reset page. Two clients: `refunds-portal-web` offers the local email-and-password sign-in and the staff sign-in broker; `loyalty-points-web` offers only the member sign-in and staff sign-in brokers. Each web app accepts only its own roles (Refunds Portal web: CUSTOMER, BRANCH_MANAGER; Loyalty Points web: MEMBER, LOYALTY_ADMINISTRATOR) and otherwise sends the person to sign in again with `prompt=login`, so a realm session never carries one app's identity into the other (R-12). Access tokens live 5 minutes and refresh through a session that ends after 30 idle minutes, so a removed staff role ends within 5 minutes (§16.8)."
  - §16.8, Suspension and revocation: "a removed staff role ends when its token expires" becomes "a removed staff role ends within the 5-minute token lifetime (ADR-07)".

### OI-11: Sign-up code sends exceed the 3-second budget that INT-02 claims

- **Where:** §12 INT-02 (Timeout), §8.5.3 Figure 9, §17.1 (Sign-up), §17.4 (Developer Notes)
- **Type:** NFR shortfall
- **Concern:** INT-02 sets "2 s when a customer waits for a code, inside the 3 s of REFUNDS/NFR-05". Figure 9 sends the email code and then the SMS code one after the other, each a separate API-14 call with its own 2-second API-05 timeout, after a Keycloak admin call that creates the user, so a slow partner gives a worst case above 4 seconds before the customer gets an answer. When the email code goes out and the SMS times out, the customer is told that codes cannot be sent ([REFUNDS/UC-06](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-06-sign-up-and-sign-in) E3) although one code arrived, and the sign-up it belongs to is deleted. The code path and the event-driven messages also share one API-05 client in notifications, so a backlog of event-driven retries during a partner slowdown can use up the capacity a waiting customer needs.
- **Options:**
  - **A.** Send both codes concurrently under one shared 2-second deadline, give the Keycloak call its own short timeout, close the whole sign-up on any failed send, and isolate the code path in its own bulkhead - fits the 3 seconds with margin; concurrent calls in customer-accounts.
  - **B.** Keep sequential sends with a 1-second timeout each - simple; a normal partner response near 1 second turns into false E3 answers.
  - **C.** Answer at once and send the codes in the background - fastest; loses the immediate E3 answer that ADR-05 exists for.
- **Recommended Answer:** Option A - four edits:
  - §12 INT-02, Timeout: "Codes: customer-accounts sends the email code and the SMS code of one sign-up concurrently through API-14 under one shared 2 s deadline, after a Keycloak user creation with a 500 ms timeout, so the sign-up answers inside the 3 s of REFUNDS/NFR-05; a new code ([REFUNDS/UC-06](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-06-sign-up-and-sign-in) E1) and a reset code ([REFUNDS/UC-06](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-06-sign-up-and-sign-in) A3) are one send with the same 2 s deadline. [NEEDS CLARIFICATION: timeout for event-driven messages]".
  - §17.1, Sign-up, append: "If either code cannot be sent, the sign-up answers E3 and is closed with its Keycloak user, so a code already sent stops working."
  - §8.5.3 Figure 9: the two API-14 calls go in one `par` block.
  - §17.4, Developer Notes, Recommended patterns, append: "API-14 sends run in their own Resilience4j bulkhead, apart from the event-driven API-05 sends."
- **Why:** §12 already commits the code wait to the REFUNDS/NFR-05 budget, so the sequence must fit it; concurrency halves the worst case without changing what the customer sees, and a separate bulkhead follows the CLAUDE.md resilience default for provider calls. The tradeoff accepted: a concurrent call pair in customer-accounts; the 500 ms value is a proposal.
- **Status:** Accepted - applied (§12 INT-02, §8.5.3 Figure 9, §17.1, §17.4)

- **Concern:** INT-05 and §17.2 refresh the branch assignments "on a schedule and at each branch manager sign-in", and alert "when they are older than one refresh interval". Sign-in happens in Keycloak through the staff sign-in broker (API-11), and nothing tells refund-requests that a sign-in took place; the interval itself is not set. ### OI-14: Items notices to POS Records can arrive out of order

- **Where:** §17.2 (Business Logic), §12 INT-03, §15.3 API-02
- **Type:** Corner case
- **Concern:** Each items notice is sent from its own event and retried on its own with backoff (INT-03, API-02). When POS Records is down at submission and the customer cancels before it recovers, the notice "items in the request" and the notice "items freed" retry side by side, and the hold can land after the release; POS Records then keeps the items blocked for a Cancelled request, and the branch cannot refund them in person, which [REFUNDS/UC-03](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-03-cancel-a-refund-request) step 5 promises ("the items can be refunded at the branch").   - **B.** Send changes with a sequence number per request that POS Records applies in order - exact history at POS Records; needs POS Records to support sequences (`TBD - external`).
  - **C.** Keep per-event changes and add a daily reconciliation against POS Records - catches errors afterwards; a blocked item can stay blocked for a day.
- **Recommended Answer:** Option A - two edits:
  - §17.2, Business Logic, new bullet: "**Items notice** (API-02; [REFUNDS/UC-01](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-01-request-a-refund) step 6, [REFUNDS/UC-03](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-03-cancel-a-refund-request) step 5, [REFUNDS/UC-04](../brd-refunds-portal/06b-use-cases-branch-manager.md#uc-04-approve--reject-refund) A2 and E1): each of the four triggering events marks the request's notice as due.   - §15.3 API-02, Purpose becomes: "Tell POS Records the current item state of a portal request, held or freed: [REFUNDS/UC-01](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-01-request-a-refund) step 6, [REFUNDS/UC-03](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-03-cancel-a-refund-request) step 5, [REFUNDS/UC-04](../brd-refunds-portal/06b-use-cases-branch-manager.md#uc-04-approve--reject-refund) A2 and E1; INT-03 in §12."
- **Why:** option A keeps the promise of [REFUNDS/UC-03](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-03-cancel-a-refund-request) step 5 and the dependency of [REFUNDS 02 § Dependencies](../brd-refunds-portal/02-glossary-assumptions-facts.md#dependencies) without waiting for the POS Records documentation, and it fits the send job proposed in OI-05; the 15-minute lag alert makes the remaining window visible. The tradeoff accepted: a state record per request and a status read before each send.
- **Status:** Accepted - applied (§17.2, §15.3 API-02)

### OI-15: Keycloak user writes are dual writes outside the outbox

- **Where:** §17.1 (Business Logic, Integrations), §8.5.3, §16.8 (Suspension and revocation)
- **Type:** Architecture gap
- **Concern:** customer-accounts writes to Keycloak and to its own schema in one request with no shared transaction: sign-up creates a disabled user, confirmation enables it with the CUSTOMER role and then creates the account, and the closure job deletes the user after it removes the contact details. The Integrations row promises "nothing is half-created: a user created for a failed sign-up is deleted", which covers a clean failure, not a crash between the two writes. A crash after enabling and before the account commits leaves an enabled CUSTOMER user with no account: the person can sign in, `GET /v1/customer-accounts/me` finds nothing, and the closure job never sees them. A closure whose Keycloak delete fails leaves a user with the email address the closure promised to remove ([REFUNDS 03 § Refund records](../brd-refunds-portal/03-definitions-and-domain-concepts.md#refund-records)). A sign-up that is simply abandoned keeps its disabled user for good; Keycloak allows one user per email address, so the same person's next sign-up fails at user creation although no account exists, where [REFUNDS/UC-06](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-06-sign-up-and-sign-in) E2 is only for "an account already exists for this email address". This is the dual-write pattern CLAUDE.md forbids, on the identity store instead of a broker.
- **Options:**
  - **A.** Compensate inline, expire abandoned sign-ups with their users, reconcile Keycloak against the module every hour, and delete closed users from the publication log - every path converges; one job and one internal listener.
  - **B.** Create the Keycloak user only at confirmation - no orphan from an abandoned sign-up; the module would have to hold the password until confirmation, which ADR-07 rules out ("credentials never reach the modules except at sign-up and password reset").
  - **C.** Accept the gaps and fix them by hand - no build cost; silent retention breaches and lockouts that operations cannot see.
- **Recommended Answer:** Option A - §17.1, Business Logic, new bullet: "**Keycloak user lifecycle:** (1) A sign-up stores the Keycloak user id it creates; if its transaction does not commit, the user is deleted at once. (2) A pending sign-up expires 24 hours after its latest code, and job (5) then deletes its disabled user. A sign-up for an email address that has a pending sign-up but no account replaces that sign-up and its disabled user, so E2 is given only when an account exists. (3) Confirmation enables the user and then commits the account; if the commit fails, the user is disabled again. (4) Closure commits the closed state and the removal of the contact details together with a `CustomerAccountClosed` publication that only customer-accounts handles (so it stays out of §14.10); its after-commit listener deletes the Keycloak user from the publication log, retried until done. (5) An hourly `keycloak-user-reconciliation` job disables any enabled CUSTOMER user with no account and deletes any disabled customer user with no pending sign-up."
- **Why:** [REFUNDS 03 § Refund records](../brd-refunds-portal/03-definitions-and-domain-concepts.md#refund-records) makes closure a removal promise and [REFUNDS/UC-06](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-06-sign-up-and-sign-in) E2 is a statement about accounts, so both must hold after a crash; option A converges without holding passwords and reuses the outbox the platform already has. The tradeoff accepted: one internal event and one more job; the 24-hour expiry is a proposal.
- **Status:** Accepted - applied (§17.1)

- **Concern:** When REFUNDS accepted the retries of [REFUNDS/UC-06](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-06-sign-up-and-sign-in) E1 (enter the code again while it still works) and the reset of [REFUNDS/UC-06](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-06-sign-up-and-sign-in) A3, it left "a limit on tries" to "the security design in the SDD" ([REFUNDS 13 OI-32](../brd-refunds-portal/13-open-items-and-clarifications.md#oi-32-sign-up-does-not-say-what-happens-when-the-customer-turns-down-an-offer), [REFUNDS 13 OI-33](../brd-refunds-portal/13-open-items-and-clarifications.md#oi-33-sign-in-has-no-failure-path-and-no-way-back-for-a-forgotten-password)). The SDD sets no limit on wrong codes per code, on new codes per address, on reset requests per account, or on failed password sign-ins ([REFUNDS/UC-06](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-06-sign-up-and-sign-in) E4); the gateway names rate limits only for sign-up, password reset, and receipt lookups (§6), not for the confirmation endpoints. The code length and its random source are not stated either.
- **Options:**
  - **A.** Limits per code, per address, and per account in customer-accounts, Keycloak brute-force detection for passwords, and gateway limits on the confirmation endpoints - closes the handed-over gap; the numbers need the security owner's confirmation.
  - **B.** Gateway rate limits per client address only - one place to configure; attackers spread over many addresses, and customers behind a shared address block each other.
- **Recommended Answer:** Option A - three edits:
  - §17.1, Codes, append: "A code has 6 digits from a cryptographically secure random source. It accepts at most 5 wrong entries; the 5th wrong entry ends it, and the customer asks for a new one ([REFUNDS/UC-06](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-06-sign-up-and-sign-in) E1). At most 5 codes per address per hour and 3 password resets per account per hour; beyond that the endpoint answers 429 `RATE_LIMITED` with a plain-language wait message."
  - §10 ADR-07, How, append: "Keycloak brute-force detection locks a customer's password sign-in for 15 minutes after 5 failed tries ([REFUNDS/UC-06](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-06-sign-up-and-sign-in) E4)."
  - §11.6, new bullet: "**Attempt limits:** the code, reset, and sign-in limits of §17.1 and ADR-07; the gateway also rate-limits `POST /v1/sign-ups/{signUpId}/confirmation`, `POST /v1/sign-ups/{signUpId}/codes`, and `POST /v1/password-resets/{passwordResetId}/confirmation` per client address."
- **Why:** the BRD delegated the limit explicitly, so leaving it out leaves a hole the BRD reviewers believe is closed; option A follows common practice for one-time codes (few tries, short life) and keeps a mistyping customer moving through [REFUNDS/UC-06](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-06-sign-up-and-sign-in) E1. The tradeoff accepted: a customer who mistypes a code five times must ask for a new one; the values are proposals for the security owner.
- **Status:** Accepted - applied (§17.1, §10 ADR-07, §11.6)

### OI-18: Two submissions on one receipt can pass the card-paid cap together

- **Where:** §17.2 (Amounts, Submission)
- **Type:** Corner case
- **Concern:** A request's amount is capped at "the card-paid amount left on the purchase after the other requests that are not Cancelled" ([REFUNDS/UC-01](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-01-request-a-refund) BR-4: refund capped at the card-paid amount). The item locks serialise requests for the same item units, but two concurrent submissions for different items of one receipt paid partly by card each read the same amount left before the other commits, and together they can exceed the card payment; two browser tabs, or two people holding the same receipt, are enough. The counting rule is also the SDD's, not the BRD's: [REFUNDS/UC-01](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-01-request-a-refund) BR-4 caps "the refund", while the SDD also counts Rejected and Payout failed requests, which pay nothing through the portal, so a rejected request can leave the customer's other items with no card amount left. Which requests count is a business rule no BRD states.
- **Options:**
  - **A.** Serialise every submission and cancellation on one purchase with a row lock on a purchase record, and keep the current counting rule as a marked question for the REFUNDS owner - closes the race; the counting question stays visible.
  - **B.** Check the cap again at approval instead of at submission - no lock at submission; the customer sees an amount at [REFUNDS/UC-01](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-01-request-a-refund) step 4 that a manager may later be unable to approve.
- **Recommended Answer:** Option A - §17.2, Amounts, append: "The cap is computed under a row lock on a purchase record keyed by (`tenant_id`, purchase reference) that every submission and every cancellation of that purchase takes in its transaction, so two requests on one receipt are decided one after the other. [NEEDS CLARIFICATION: whether a Rejected or Payout failed request still counts against the card-paid amount; [REFUNDS/UC-01](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-01-request-a-refund) BR-4 caps the refund but does not say. Until answered, every request that is not Cancelled counts.]"
- **Why:** [REFUNDS/UC-01](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-01-request-a-refund) BR-4 is a money limit (REFUNDS/NFR-01), so it must hold under concurrency, and the lock costs nothing at about 40 requests a day (§18.1); the counting rule decides how much a customer can get back and belongs to the REFUNDS owner, while the current conservative rule never pays more than the card share. The tradeoff accepted: one lock row per purchase.
- **Status:** Accepted - applied (§17.2)

### OI-19: Retrying a refused payout with the same idempotency key may never succeed

- **Where:** §17.3 (Retry), §12 INT-01, §15.3 API-03, §4 R-07
- **Type:** Risk
- **Concern:** §17.3 retries "a refusal, an error, or a timeout" with "the same idempotency key" on every try until the deadline. Payment APIs that honour idempotency keys commonly return the stored result of the first request for a repeated key; with such a provider a refusal repeated under the same key is replayed, not retried, and the later success [REFUNDS/UC-04](../brd-refunds-portal/06b-use-cases-branch-manager.md#uc-04-approve--reject-refund) E1 expects ("If a later try succeeds, the flow goes on at step 7") cannot happen; [REFUNDS 16](../brd-refunds-portal/16-uat-bat-test-cases.md) REFUNDS/TC-DEC-09 tests exactly that case (refused for 23 hours, then accepted). A new key on every try would instead let the provider pay twice after an ambiguous timeout (R-07). The design does not separate a definitive refusal from an ambiguous outcome, and R-07 only asks whether CardPay honours keys at all.
- **Options:**
  - **A.** One open attempt at a time, each with its own key: an ambiguous outcome (timeout, 5xx, no answer) is resent with the same attempt key or resolved by a status query, and only a definitive refusal closes the attempt and opens the next with a new key - retries can succeed and never pay twice; depends on CardPay telling refusals from errors (`TBD - external`).
  - **B.** The same key for every try, as now - no double payment; refusals never turn into a success, so every refusal ends Payout failed.
  - **C.** A new key on every try - refusals can succeed; a timed-out try that the provider did process is paid again.
- **Recommended Answer:** Option A - three edits:
  - §17.3, Retry becomes: "**Retry** ([REFUNDS/UC-04](../brd-refunds-portal/06b-use-cases-branch-manager.md#uc-04-approve--reject-refund) E1): each try belongs to an attempt with its own key (payout id and attempt number), and at most one attempt is open. A timeout, a 5xx, or no answer leaves the attempt open: the next try resends it with the same key, or asks the provider for its status when API-03 offers that. Only a definitive refusal closes the attempt, and the next try opens a new attempt with a new key. So a refusal can still be followed by a success, and an outcome the platform did not see is never paid again (REFUNDS/NFR-01)."
  - §4, R-07, Mitigation: "One open attempt per payout, the same key while its outcome is unknown, a new key only after a definitive refusal (§17.3); which CardPay answers are definitive refusals, and whether it replays the stored result for a reused key, is `TBD - external` (API-03)."
  - §15.3 API-03, the TBD - EXTERNAL marker, append: "which answers are definitive refusals; whether a reused key replays the stored result; whether a payout status query exists".
- **Why:** option A is the only one that satisfies both halves of [REFUNDS/UC-04](../brd-refunds-portal/06b-use-cases-branch-manager.md#uc-04-approve--reject-refund) E1 (a later try can succeed) and REFUNDS/NFR-01 (never paid twice); its dependency on CardPay's answers is already an open `TBD - external` question, and if CardPay cannot tell refusals from errors, B becomes the fallback with the business told that refusals end Payout failed. The tradeoff accepted: attempt bookkeeping in payouts.
- **Status:** Accepted - applied (§17.3, §4 R-07, §15.3 API-03)

Tell the branch manager and any active cover of the branch before they settle the refund in person. 3. If the branch has already settled, record both payments and hand the case to the REFUNDS owner. [NEEDS CLARIFICATION: who contacts the branch, and the response time.]"
- **Why:** option B rewrites a BRD lifecycle for a rare case, while A keeps the lifecycle and stops the loss where it happens, at the branch counter; REFUNDS/NFR-01 counts even one duplicate payout as a failure, so the case needs a risk owner and a procedure. The tradeoff accepted: a human step on a rare page.

### OI-21: Purchase and refund facts for one purchase can race in the points ledger

- **Where:** §17.5 (Earning, Take-back, Corrections), Figure 28
- **Type:** Corner case
- **Concern:** A paid refund whose purchase does not show yet "waits and is applied when the purchase shows" ([LOYALTY/UC-02](../brd-loyalty-points/06a-use-cases-member.md#uc-02-view-points-history) BR-5: take-back recorded when the purchase shows). The `RefundPaid` listener checks whether the purchase shows, and the API-07 handler checks whether a refund waits; when both run at the same moment, each transaction misses the other's uncommitted row, the refund is stored as waiting, the purchase earns, and no step ever applies the take-back, so the balance stays too high (LOYALTY/NFR-01). The same check-then-act gap lets a correction for a missing purchase ([LOYALTY/UC-03](../brd-loyalty-points/06b-use-cases-loyalty-administrator.md#uc-03-correct-a-members-points) E4: purchase already shows) and a POS Records report of that purchase for the same member both earn, which [LOYALTY/UC-03](../brd-loyalty-points/06b-use-cases-loyalty-administrator.md#uc-03-correct-a-members-points) BR-4 forbids (the corrected purchase counts as shown). Optimistic locking on the member's balance does not close it, because the two steps can write different rows.
- **Options:**
  - **A.** Serialise every step on one purchase with a row lock on a purchase record keyed by tenant and purchase reference, inserted when absent, plus a daily check for waiting refunds whose purchase now shows - closes both races; one lock per purchase step.
  - **B.** Rely on a periodic sweep that applies waiting refunds - simple; the take-back can be later than the hour of LOYALTY/NFR-02, and the correction race stays open.
- **Recommended Answer:** Option A - §17.5, Business Logic, new bullet: "**One purchase at a time:** every step on one purchase (an API-07 report, a `RefundPaid` take-back, applying a waiting refund, a correction that names a missing purchase) first takes a row lock on the purchase record keyed by (`tenant_id`, purchase reference), inserting it when absent, in the same transaction, so each step sees the committed result of the one before it. A daily `waiting-refund-check` job applies any waiting refund whose purchase shows and raises an alert for each one it finds, as a check that the lock works."
- **Why:** LOYALTY/NFR-01 accepts zero mismatches in acceptance tests, and a race that leaves a balance too high would surface only as a member complaint; the lock is cheap because few steps touch one purchase reference. The tradeoff accepted: a lock row per purchase reference and one more job.
- **Status:** Accepted - applied (§17.5)

### OI-44: The `movement_id` key blocks the former-member deletion when a kept refund application names a movement of the ended period

- **Where:** §17.5 (Retention Policy: Former-member history; Tables Design: `refund_application` `movement_id`; the membership model under Figure 26; Error Handling), §20.1.16
- **Type:** Corner case
- **Concern:** OI-43 made `refund_application.movement_id` a foreign key to `points_movement` and appended the deletion order to the Former-member history bullet of the Retention Policy: "Each deletion removes the rows that refer to others first: the refund applications, then the movements, then the purchases, the refunds left with no application, and the period." The applications it removes are those of the purchases it deletes ("the member's `purchase` rows dated in that period, or after it and before the day the member next rejoined (HELD purchases included), with their `refund_application` rows"), while "Every movement belongs to the period it was recorded in" and notices are "applied in date order, so a notice that arrives late lands at its date" (the membership model under Figure 26). When POS Records reports a purchase and its refund is paid while the platform still counts the person as a member, and leave and rejoin notices dated on or before that purchase land only afterwards, the purchase is dated in the new membership and keeps its refund application ("`purchase`, `refund_application`, `points_movement` of current members: kept while the person is a member"), but the Taken back movement that application names stays in the ended period. 24 months after the leave, `former-member-retention` reaches that movement, the key refuses its deletion, and the member's transaction fails; Error Handling retries it "at the next run", where it fails again, every night. Before the key, this deletion completed and left the application's `movement_id` naming a deleted row, which OI-43 set out to prevent; now the whole ended period of that member, with its HELD purchases and notices, outlives the 24 months of [LOYALTY 03 § Membership end and data retention](../brd-loyalty-points/03-definitions-and-domain-concepts.md#membership-end-and-data-retention) and the legitimate-interests basis that §17.5 Compliance limits to "within the retention period". If the new membership ends too, its own deletion meets the `purchase_id` key of the movements left in the ended period, so, as each deletion is written, neither period is ever deleted. `retention_overdue_rows` raises the alert, but step 2 of §20.1.16, "Fix the cause; the next daily run deletes the rows", has no designed fix for this cause, so the operator must choose a change to production data that no rule states. The case predates v1.6 and stays in the Reviewer Notes ("Considered and not raised ([2026-10-07] delta, outside its scope)"); what OI-43 adds is a designed deletion that can no longer complete.
- **Options:**
  - **A.** Before it deletes the period's movements, the deletion clears `movement_id` in each refund application it keeps that names one of them, and the column's null case says so - the deletion completes on time with both keys in place, and nothing a member or the Loyalty Administrator sees changes, since the ended period's movements are never shown and the application keeps its `points_taken_back`; a kept application can then name no movement although it took points back.
  - **B.** When a late leave or rejoin notice lands, move the movements of each purchase dated on or after the rejoin day from the ended period into the new one, with their points in its balance - no movement of the new membership stays in the ended period, so the deletion never meets the case, and the member keeps the points that [LOYALTY 03 § Membership end and data retention](../brd-loyalty-points/03-definitions-and-domain-concepts.md#membership-end-and-data-retention) gives a purchase made on or after the rejoin day; it rewrites append-only movements and balances, and it repairs the pre-existing case that the Reviewer Notes leave for the owner's review of §17.5.
  - **C.** Keep the text and add a step for this cause to §20.1.16 - no design change; the deletion fails every night until an operator changes production data under the elevation of §19, and the rows stay past their period meanwhile.
- **Recommended Answer:** Option A - two edits in §17.5:
  - Retention Policy, the Former-member history bullet, append after the deletion-order sentence: "A refund application that the deletion keeps, because its purchase is dated in a later membership, but that names one of the period's movements has its `movement_id` cleared before the movements are deleted."
  - Tables Design, the `refund_application` `movement_id` row, Constraints: "NULL when nothing was taken back" becomes "NULL when nothing was taken back, or after the movement it named was deleted with former-member history (Retention Policy)".
- **Why:** the OI-43 deletion order holds for every row inside the deletion set, but in the late-notice case a kept application outside it names a movement inside it, and Error Handling only retries, so the key turns a deletion that completed into one that never does, against the 24 months of [LOYALTY 03 § Membership end and data retention](../brd-loyalty-points/03-definitions-and-domain-concepts.md#membership-end-and-data-retention) and the §17.5 Compliance basis that ends with the period; option A keeps both keys and the one deletion order, changes nothing a member or the Loyalty Administrator sees, and adds no business behaviour, while B repairs the pre-existing case (the purchase's points left in the ended period) at a cost that belongs to the owner's review of §17.5, and C leaves personal data past its period until an operator acts. The tradeoff accepted: a kept refund application that took points back can name no movement, while its `points_taken_back` still records them.
- **Status:** Accepted - applied (§17.5 Retention Policy, Tables Design)
