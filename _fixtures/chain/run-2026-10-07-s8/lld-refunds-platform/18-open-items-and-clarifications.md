### OI-09: The last-period deletion is blocked by member rows outside the deletion set

- **Where:** loyalty-points §7.3 ([notice and retention](./04-implementation/loyalty-points.md#loyaltypointsserviceimplnotice-and-retention), former-member-retention step 4); 05 §8.6; 13 §16.3
- **Type:** Missing edge case
- **Concern:** Step 4 deletes "with the last period, points_balance and member too", 05 §8.6 says that with every loyalty key at NO ACTION "that order, never a cascade, removes the referring rows", and the new 13 §16.3 case asserts that "no key fails the member's transaction". Two cases break this. First, each period's set is the [SDD Retention Policy](../sdd-refunds-platform/13e-service-loyalty-points.md#retention-policy) set, purchases "dated in that period, or after it and before the day the member next rejoined", but the [SDD membership model](../sdd-refunds-platform/13e-service-loyalty-points.md#business-logic) gives a member first seen through a rejoin notice a first period that starts on the rejoin day, and Earning stores every reported purchase for its member, adding no movement for one dated before that day; such a purchase, reported after the notice (a late POS delivery, or a purchase dated before go-live), is in no period's set, so it and any refund application recorded for it outlive every period and the member delete fails on `purchase.member_id`. Second, "for each period 24 months after the member left" sets no order, and the SDD v1.6 review saw, outside its delta, that "nothing orders two overdue deletions of one member after an unrelated failure": with two periods due in one run, deleting the newer one first, and the member with it, fails on `membership_period.member_id`. Either way the member's one transaction rolls back every night, the ended period's personal data outlives the 24 months of [LOYALTY 03 § Membership end and data retention](../brd-loyalty-points/03-definitions-and-domain-concepts.md#membership-end-and-data-retention), and `retention_overdue_rows` sends the operator to [SDD §20.1.16](../sdd-refunds-platform/16-operations-runbook.md#20116-retention-overdue), whose "Fix the cause" step has no designed fix: the failure mode SDD OI-44 removed for `movement_id`.
- **Options:**
  - **A.** In §7.3, delete a member's due periods oldest first in its one transaction; add to the first period's set the member's purchases dated before that period's start date (a first period opened by a rejoin notice), with their refund applications and the refunds left with none; delete `points_balance` and `member` only when no `membership_period` of the member remains; add both cases to the 13 §16.3 retention test; hand the set wording to sdd-unifier - every due deletion completes and no row names a deleted member; the LLD reads the SDD set for a case the SDD does not name until the SDD confirms it.
  - **B.** Keep the SDD set and skip the `member` and `points_balance` delete while a purchase still names the member - no key fails; the member number, those purchases and their refund rows stay indefinitely, against LOYALTY 03.
  - **C.** Keep the text and leave both cases to `retention_overdue_rows` - no change; the period's history outlives its 24 months until an operator edits production data under no stated rule.
- **Recommendation:** A; oldest-first deletion of due periods, the pre-start purchases in the first period's set, `member` and `points_balance` deleted after the last remaining period, two more test cases, and the Retention Policy wording raised through sdd-unifier.
- **Why:** SDD 13e deletes `member` and `points_balance` "with their last membership period", and this LLD keeps every key at NO ACTION (§7.3, 05 §8.6), so no row may still name the member at that point, and LOYALTY 03 deletes a former member's history 24 months after they leave; a purchase dated before the first known period comes from a time that ended before that period began, so deleting it with that period never shortens its retention, and oldest-first order makes "the last period" the last remaining one. The tradeoff accepted: an LLD reading of the SDD set, pending its confirmation upstream, and two more integration cases.
- **Status:** Resolved - accepted recommendation applied in 1.3

### OI-10: Step 4 has no rule for the notices that opened or closed a period

- **Where:** loyalty-points §7.3 ([notice and retention](./04-implementation/loyalty-points.md#loyaltypointsserviceimplnotice-and-retention): former-member-retention step 4 and the notice replay); 13 §16.3
- **Type:** Missing edge case
- **Concern:** Step 4 deletes "the period with the notices that opened or closed it", but a `membership_notice` row names no period (its key columns are member number, type and `effective_on`, 05 §8.2), and the [SDD membership model](../sdd-refunds-platform/13e-service-loyalty-points.md#business-logic) opens a period on the rejoin day "or from the next day when it is dated on the day of leaving". After a same-day leave and rejoin ([LOYALTY/TC-BAL-10](../brd-loyalty-points/16-uat-bat-test-cases.md#2-points-balance-uc-01-mk-01--lp-01-nfr-03), [LOYALTY/TC-HIS-26](../brd-loyalty-points/16-uat-bat-test-cases.md#3-points-history-uc-02-mk-02--lp-02-nfr-02)), the REJOIN notice that opened the kept period is dated on the ended period's last day: matching notices by the ended period's date range deletes it, so a later reconciliation that replays the stored notices in effective-date order (the §7.3 notice path) has lost the notice that opened the current membership, while matching a REJOIN only to the period whose `starts_on` equals its date never deletes it, so it outlives the member. A notice that opened or closed no period (for example a second LEAVE, with another date, for a member already former) has no deletion rule in the [SDD Retention Policy](../sdd-refunds-platform/13e-service-loyalty-points.md#retention-policy) at all. Each leak keeps a member number, personal data in the 13e Data Encryption list, after the member row is gone and past the 24 months of LOYALTY 03.
- **Options:**
  - **A.** State the match in §7.3: a LEAVE closed the period whose `ends_on` is its date; a REJOIN opened the period that starts on its date, or on the next day when its date is the previous period's `ends_on`; delete exactly those notices with each period, and with the member's last remaining period every remaining notice of that member number; add a same-day leave and rejoin case to the 13 §16.3 retention test (the kept period's REJOIN survives the ended period's deletion); hand the case of a notice that opened or closed no period to sdd-unifier - follows the SDD model with no schema change and leaves no notice past its member; one date rule that the notice path and the job must share.
  - **B.** Link each notice to the period it opened or closed with a new column - an exact match; a column SDD 13e Tables Design does not have, so an SDD change first and a migration of every notice writer.
  - **C.** Leave the match to the implementer - no text; either a current membership loses its opening notice or notices outlive the member.
- **Recommendation:** A; the same-day aware match, the last-period sweep of the member's notices, one more test case, and the uncovered notice case raised through sdd-unifier.
- **Why:** SDD 13e already fixes the same-day rule and that a notice is "deleted with the membership period it opened or closed", so A derives the match without changing an SDD-owned table, and the sweep ends the member number with the member row, as LOYALTY 03 and the 13e PII list require; B is exact but changes the SDD schema. The tradeoff accepted: a date rule kept in step between the notice reconciliation and the retention job, and one more test case.
- **Status:** Resolved - accepted recommendation applied in 1.3

### OI-11: Retention deletes purchase_lock rows that the lock acquisition assumes exist

- **Where:** loyalty-points §7.3 ([recordPurchase, applyRefund and correct](./04-implementation/loyalty-points.md#loyaltypointsserviceimplrecordpurchase-applyrefund-and-correct): the purchase-lock rule; [notice and retention](./04-implementation/loyalty-points.md#loyaltypointsserviceimplnotice-and-retention): former-member-retention and waiting-refund-check); 13 §16.3
- **Type:** Concurrency hazard
- **Concern:** Every purchase-related path "inserts purchase_lock if absent then locks it first", and the new retention text deletes the lock row itself ("purchase_lock goes with the last purchase and refund of its reference"), in former-member-retention, which states no lock, and in waiting-refund-check, which works "under the purchase lock". Under PostgreSQL READ COMMITTED, a `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE` that waits on a row whose deleting transaction then commits returns no row, and the insert-if-absent step before it found the row present and inserted nothing, so a POS report or a missing-purchase correction on that purchase reference ends its acquisition holding no lock: it fails if the code checks, or runs unserialized if it does not, against SDD 13e One purchase at a time ("so each step sees the committed result of the one before it"). The window is small (a step on a reference at the moment its last rows are deleted) and mostly self-healing, but the acquisition is the module's only serialization of a purchase and the pseudocode leaves its behaviour under deletion to each repository call; the gap is a missing rule, not the generic verification CONFIRM-05 asks for.
- **Options:**
  - **A.** Acquire the purchase lock with one statement that always ends holding a row (an `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT (tenant_id, purchase_reference) DO UPDATE` no-op, which PostgreSQL resolves to an insert or an update under concurrency, or an insert-then-lock loop until the row comes back), and let both jobs delete a `purchase_lock` row only while holding it, after rechecking that no purchase or refund of its reference remains; add a Testcontainers barrier case (a job deletes the last purchase and refund of a reference while a POS report of that reference for another member waits; the report ends holding a lock row and records its purchase once) - the lock survives the deletion the Retention Policy requires; one PostgreSQL-specific statement in the repository.
  - **B.** Never delete `purchase_lock` rows - no race; keeps purchase references, personal data in the 13e Data Encryption list, against the 13e Retention Policy.
  - **C.** Leave it to implementation - no text; whether a waiter fails or runs unlocked depends on how each repository call is written.
- **Recommendation:** A; an always-locking acquisition, lock-row deletion only under that lock, and one barrier test.
- **Why:** SDD 13e makes the purchase lock the serialization of every step on one purchase and its Retention Policy deletes the lock row, so the acquisition must tolerate that deletion; A keeps the purchase-first order of OI-03's resolution (no member lock is held while a purchase lock is taken) and adds no new lock order. The tradeoff accepted: a database-specific acquisition statement and a recheck in each job before the row goes.
- **Status:** Resolved - accepted recommendation applied in 1.3

### OI-14: A complete sign-in read that is not applied to an account can still close it

- **Where:** customer-accounts [§7.3](./04-implementation/customer-accounts.md#customeraccountsserviceimplreset-and-scheduled-reconciliation) (daily closure) and [Workflow: account-closure](./04-implementation/customer-accounts.md#workflow-account-closure); 13 [§16.3](./13-testing.md#163-integration-test-conventions)
- **Type:** Transaction boundary
- **Concern:** The 1.4 closure first reads the Keycloak sign-in events "and update[s] each account's last_sign_in_at from them". Then, "After a complete read only: Lock account; close only after two years without sign-in and linked_request_count == 0". Only an incomplete read stops the run. Nothing covers a complete read that fails to reach one account. [SDD 13a Error Handling](../sdd-refunds-platform/13a-service-customer-accounts.md#error-handling) has the job "work one account per transaction; a failing account is logged by its id and retried at the next run". So when one account's update fails (a lock timeout, a version conflict, a database error), that account keeps its stale value, and the same run selects it on that value and closes it. The closure removes the email address, the mobile number and the Keycloak user, which [SDD OI-57](../sdd-refunds-platform/18-open-items-and-clarifications.md#oi-57-the-account-closure-job-has-no-rule-for-a-sign-in-event-read-that-fails-or-times-out) records cannot be undone. Here it hits a customer who signed in within the last 7 days, against [REFUNDS 03 § Refund records](../brd-refunds-portal/03-definitions-and-domain-concepts.md#refund-records) ("The portal closes an account with no sign-in for 2 years and no request still linked to it"). The update rule also does not keep the later time, so an event older than a [REFUNDS/UC-06](../brd-refunds-portal/06a-use-cases-customer.md#uc-06-sign-up-and-sign-in) step 6 or A3 stamp can move last_sign_in_at back. The only closure test, `accountClosure_signInEventReadIncomplete_closesNoAccount`, covers the incomplete read. This is a missing rule, not the generic verification CONFIRM-02 asks for.
- **Options:**
  - **A.** Apply the read inside each closure decision. In the account's closure transaction, after the account lock, set last_sign_in_at to the later of its stored value and the newest sign-in the read returned for its Keycloak user, then apply the two-year rule. Every update keeps the later of the two times - no account is judged on a value the run failed to write; one more in-memory lookup per candidate.
  - **B.** Stop the tenant's closure step when any last_sign_in_at update of the run failed, as for an incomplete read - simple; one account that keeps failing blocks every closure of its tenant, and the read-age gauge does not show it.
  - **C.** Keep the text - no change; an irreversible closure depends on one row update succeeding.
- **Recommendation:** A.
  - In customer-accounts §7.3 daily closure, replace "and update each account's last_sign_in_at from them." with "and update each account's last_sign_in_at to the later of its stored value and its newest read sign-in, one account per transaction (a failing account is logged by its id)."
  - Replace "Lock account; close only after two years without sign-in and linked_request_count == 0." with "Lock account; in the same transaction set last_sign_in_at to the later of its stored value and the newest sign-in the read returned for its Keycloak user, then close only after two years without sign-in and linked_request_count == 0, so an account whose earlier update failed is judged on the read, never on a stale value."
  - In 13 §16.3 add `accountClosure_lastSignInUpdateFailedEarlierInRun_keepsAccountActive` and `accountClosure_eventOlderThanStoredSignIn_keepsStoredSignIn`.
- **Why:** REFUNDS 03 allows a closure only after 2 years with no sign-in, and the closure cannot be undone (SDD OI-57). SDD 13a's per-account failure rule lets a run go on after one account fails, so the closure decision itself must use the sign-ins the run already holds. A keeps one account per transaction, as 13a states, and needs no upstream change. B lets one bad row stop a tenant's closures with no alert. The tradeoff accepted: each closure candidate is checked against the in-memory read once more.
- **Status:** Resolved - accepted recommendation applied in 1.4

Saving events, the saved event types and their expiry are realm settings.

The new rule counts that as a complete read: [SDD OI-60](../sdd-refunds-platform/18-open-items-and-clarifications.md#oi-60-the-read-age-gauge-counts-from-a-time-that-13a-records-nowhere) has "a day with no customer sign-in is still a complete read".     - Add Keycloak container tests.
    - Tradeoff: a misconfigured realm stops the closure instead of closing active accounts; one more admin call per run.
  - **B.** Add only the 10 §13.1 row and the container test - configuration plus a build check; a realm changed after release is not detected, and the closure runs on stored values.
  - **C.** Keep the text - the 7-day retention the closure depends on is left to whoever sets up the realm.
- **Recommendation:** A.

  - In 13 §16.3 add `accountClosure_signInThroughKeycloakPage_keepsAccountWithStaleStoredSignIn` and `accountClosure_realmNotSavingSignInEvents_closesNoAccount`, both against the Keycloak container.

The tradeoff accepted: one admin read of the realm settings per run, and a row the realm export must match.

### OI-16: keycloak-user-reconciliation can delete a sign-up's new user or disable a just-confirmed customer

- **Where:** customer-accounts [§7.3](./04-implementation/customer-accounts.md#customeraccountsserviceimplreset-and-scheduled-reconciliation) (hourly reconcile) and [startSignUp and confirm](./04-implementation/customer-accounts.md#customeraccountsserviceimplstartsignup-and-confirm); [Workflow: keycloak-user-reconciliation](./04-implementation/customer-accounts.md#workflow-keycloak-user-reconciliation); 13 [§16.3](./13-testing.md#163-integration-test-conventions)
- **Type:** Concurrency hazard
- **Concern:**
  - The 1.4 text runs the hourly job as check-then-act with no transaction around its Keycloak calls: "disable enabled users without accounts; delete orphan disabled users ... the account's own transaction runs before or after the call, never around it". Nothing orders it against two requests that change the same users with no transaction open:
    - startSignUp creates the disabled user before its sign_up row commits ("With no DB transaction, create a disabled Keycloak user ... persist hashes").
    - confirm enables the user before the account commits ("then enable the Keycloak user. Commit account + COMPLETED sign-up").
  - **First window (sign-up):** the job sees a disabled customer user that no pending sign-up names and deletes it ([SDD 13a Business Logic](../sdd-refunds-platform/13a-service-customer-accounts.md#business-logic), Keycloak user lifecycle item 5). The codes then go out for a deleted user, and the confirmation answers 503 on every retry until the customer starts a new sign-up.
  - **Second window (confirmation):** the job sees an enabled CUSTOMER user with no account and disables it. The account then commits ACTIVE naming that user, and the confirmation's token call fails. The next hourly run finds a disabled user whose sign-up is COMPLETED, so no pending sign-up names it, and deletes it. The ACTIVE account now names a deleted Keycloak user, sign-in and the password reset ("update Keycloak password") fail for good, and no job restores it, because the job only disables and deletes.
  - The word "orphan" also loosens the SDD condition: an implementer who reads it as "no account" deletes the user of every pending sign-up.
  - The windows are short, but they recur every hour and the second outcome is permanent. CONFIRM-02 asks only for generic verification, not for this rule.
- **Options:**
  - **A.** Serialize per customer user with the [§12.2](./09-cross-cutting.md#122-idempotency) bounded operation guard, keyed by tenant and lower(email):
    - startSignUp holds it from the user creation until its sign_up row commits or the user is deleted.
    - confirm holds it from the enable call until the account commits or the user is disabled again.
    - The job tries the guard per user without blocking and skips a held user. Under the guard it re-reads whether an account (enabled user) or a pending sign-up (disabled user) names the user, and only then calls Keycloak.
    - Tradeoff: SDD 13a's two conditions stay exactly as written, and a crash still frees the guard with its connection; the guard is held across one Keycloak call, and the job needs each user's tenant (OI-17).
  - **B.** Narrow the job's targets without a guard: skip recently created users, enabled users that a pending sign-up names, and disabled users that an ACTIVE account names, and re-check after each write - no lock; but these conditions are not in SDD 13a item 5, and a confirmation that crashes after the enable leaves an enabled user with no account until its sign-up expires, so it needs sdd-unifier first.
  - **C.** Keep the text - no change; rare, but the second race leaves a customer unable to sign in for good.
- **Recommendation:** A.
  - In customer-accounts §7.3, replace "hourly reconcile: disable enabled users without accounts; delete orphan disabled users." with "hourly reconcile (SDD 13a Keycloak user lifecycle, item 5): for each customer user of the tenant, try the §12.2 guard keyed by tenant and lower(email), and skip the user this run when it is held; under the guard, disable an enabled CUSTOMER user only when no account names it, and delete a disabled customer user only when no pending sign-up names it."
  - Add to startSignUp: "hold that guard from the Keycloak user creation until the sign_up row commits or the user is deleted".
  - Add to confirm: "hold it from the enable call until the account commits or the user is disabled again".
  - In 13 §16.3 add the barrier cases `keycloakUserReconciliation_confirmationBetweenEnableAndCommit_leavesUserEnabled` and `keycloakUserReconciliation_signUpBeforeRowCommits_keepsDisabledUser`.
- **Why:** In SDD 13a, the requests and the job change the same Keycloak users with no transaction across the call. The LLD's own §12.2 guard is already its mechanism for serializing work that spans an external effect without holding a transaction. A keeps both SDD conditions and the crash safety net. B changes when the safety net acts, so it needs an upstream decision. The tradeoff accepted: a pooled connection held for one Keycloak call under the guard, and a busy user skipped for an hour.
- **Status:** Resolved - accepted recommendation applied in 1.4

  - **C.** Keep the text - correct only while one tenant exists.

  - In §7.2 add an outbound adapter row for `IdentityProviderPort`, whose adapter wraps the Keycloak admin client and token endpoint. Because the realm is shared, the tenant must be readable on every user the job may change, a disabled one included. A keeps the per-tenant runs that SDD 13a states; B breaks the cross-tenant rule. The tradeoff accepted: the tenant attribute is written at creation as well as at confirmation.
- **Status:** Resolved - accepted recommendation applied in 1.4

- **Where:** refund-requests [§7.3](./04-implementation/refund-requests.md#posnoticeworker-and-branch-jobs) (staff refresh); 03 [§6.4](./03-architecture.md#64-architectural-style---as-operationalised); 10 [§13.7](./10-operations.md#137-alerts); 13 [§16.3](./13-testing.md#163-integration-test-conventions)

    - Tradeoff: each read changes only what it read, a session reads API-06 at most once, and a failing scheduled refresh always alerts; one token claim to name.
  - **B.** Make the session read a full tenant refresh - one code path and one meaning of `synced_at`; every new staff session waits on a whole-tenant API-06 read inside its first request.
  - **C.** Keep the text - each implementer picks a scope and a gauge, and the wrong pick removes every other manager's branches for up to 5 minutes.

  - In 13 §16.3 add `branchQueue_firstRequestOfSession_replacesOnlyThatStaffIdsAssignments`, `branchQueue_sessionReadFails_answersFromLastSyncedCopyAndReadsOnce` and `branchAssignmentRefresh_failsWhileSessionReadsSucceed_raisesAgeAlert`.

The INT-05 alert exists to catch a stale copy, and only the oldest row shows it. A session that cannot read API-06 should not pay the timeout on every page. ### OI-27: The first-session assignment write has no transaction, no lock against the other writes, and no `synced_at` rule

- **Where:** refund-requests [§7.3](./04-implementation/refund-requests.md#posnoticeworker-and-branch-jobs) (staff refresh) and [§7.6](./04-implementation/refund-requests.md#76-transaction-boundaries); 13 [§16.3](./13-testing.md#163-integration-test-conventions)
- **Type:** Concurrency hazard
- **Concern:** The OI-19 text gives `branch_assignment` two write paths. The scheduled refresh replaces the tenant's whole set "in one transaction, every row stamped synced_at". The first branch request of a session reads API-06 for its staff id, then "replace[s] only that staff id's rows, and insert[s] the staff_session row (ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING)". The second write is missing three rules.
  - (1) Transaction: the first branch request is usually a read, which §7.6 runs "REQUIRED read-only". The text gives the write no transaction of its own, so it either fails inside the read-only transaction or turns that read into a read-write transaction, against §7.6.
  - (2) Lock: neither write locks anything first, and [SDD 13b Tables Design](../sdd-refunds-platform/13b-service-refund-requests.md#tables-design) gives `branch_assignment` no unique key besides `id`. Under READ COMMITTED, a DELETE does not see rows that another transaction inserted after the statement began. So two overlapping writes each delete the old rows and insert their own, and the staff id's rows stay twice until the next scheduled refresh. The overlap can be a session write against the scheduled replace, or two first requests of one session that a page sends together (both find no `staff_session` row: the race OI-19 named). [SDD API-13](../sdd-refunds-platform/11-api-contracts.md#api-13-get-a-branchs-recipients-notifications---refund-requests) then returns that manager twice, against its "manager and any cover", and any reader that joins the copy without collapsing rows lists that staff id's branches twice.
  - (3) Stamp: `synced_at` is NOT NULL, and the text does not say what the session write stores. The age gauge counts from the tenant's oldest `synced_at`, which OI-19 meant only a successful scheduled read to move. If session rows carry their read time, then in a tenant whose staff all start sessions within the alert interval (as in a test with one staff id), session reads keep the oldest stamp fresh while the scheduled refresh fails, and the INT-05 alert does not fire. That is the case `branchAssignmentRefresh_failsWhileSessionReadsSucceed_raisesAgeAlert` is there to pin.
- **Options:**
  - **A.** Give the session write one short transaction of its own before the request's transaction, have both writes take one per-tenant lock first, and stamp session rows with the tenant's oldest `synced_at` - no duplicate rows, and only the scheduled refresh moves the age; a session write can wait for a running scheduled replace, and its rows look as old as the last scheduled refresh.
  - **B.** Add a unique key on (`tenant_id`, `branch_id`, `staff_id`, `role`, `valid_from`) and a per-tenant record of the last good scheduled refresh for the gauge - enforced by the schema; both change SDD 13b Tables Design, so sdd-unifier first.
  - **C.** Keep the text - duplicate rows for up to 5 minutes, and whatever stamp an implementer picks decides whether session reads can hold down the age alert.
- **Recommendation:** A.
  - In refund-requests §7.3, replace "replace only that staff id's rows, and insert the staff_session row (ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING) whether the read succeeded or fell back to the last synced copy." with "then, in one short transaction before the request's own transaction, replace only that staff id's rows, each stamped with the tenant's oldest synced_at (the read time when the tenant has none), and insert the staff_session row (ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING), whether the read succeeded or fell back to the last synced copy. The scheduled replace and this write each first take a transaction-scoped advisory lock keyed by tenant, so neither misses the rows of the other."
  - In §7.6, add `| First-session assignment write | REQUIRED, own transaction before the request's transaction | READ_COMMITTED + per-tenant advisory lock | Rollback keeps the last synced copy and inserts no staff_session row; the request answers from the copy |`.
  - In 13 §16.3, add `branchAssignmentRefresh_overlapsFirstSessionWrite_keepsOneRowPerAssignment`.
- **Why:** In SDD 13b the decision gate and API-13 read this copy, and the Metrics count the gauge from the last good refresh, which OI-19 tied to the scheduled read. So the copy must stay one row per assignment, and the stamp must not move the age. A closes all three gaps inside the LLD with the advisory lock §12.2 already uses for its guard; B needs two SDD table changes.
