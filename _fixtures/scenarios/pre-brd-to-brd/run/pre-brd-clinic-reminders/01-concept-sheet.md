# Concept Sheet

A concise document that outlines the core idea of a product, project, or business initiative. It is used to present a proposal at an early stage to stakeholders, clients, investors, or internal teams before developing a full business plan or prototype.

| Section | What to capture | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Title / Name | A short, clear name for the concept (for example, product name). | Clinic Reminders |
| Overview / Summary | A 2 to 4 line high-level description of the concept: what it is, what problem it solves, and for whom. | A monthly subscription for small private clinics in Cairo and Giza that reminds patients of appointments on WhatsApp (SMS when WhatsApp is not delivered), lets them confirm or cancel with one reply, offers freed slots to the clinic's waitlist, and sends the owner a weekly no-show report. It turns silent no-shows into early cancellations and refilled slots without reminder calls. |
| Problem Statement | Clearly state the gap or pain point in the market that this idea addresses. Include data if possible. | Small private clinics lose paid doctor time when patients do not come or cancel without notice. Outpatient no-shows average about 23% across 105 studies worldwide and forgetting is a leading stated cause (evidence in [10-efas.md](10-efas.md), O1); in Egypt 57.2% of health spending is out of pocket (2023), so an empty slot in a private clinic is lost cash ([08-pestle-analysis.md](08-pestle-analysis.md), Economic). Receptionists remind patients by phone or personal WhatsApp, cancellations arrive late or never, and freed slots stay empty because waiting patients cannot be reached in time. No baseline exists yet for Greater Cairo private clinics (open in 10, O1). |
| Proposed Solution / Idea Description | A detailed explanation of the idea, product, or service: what it does, how it works, and why it matters. | Reception enters or imports appointments in a web dashboard. At a set time before each visit (default 24 hours), the service sends an approved Arabic or English WhatsApp utility template with Confirm and Cancel buttons; if WhatsApp is not delivered, an SMS with a short confirm or cancel link follows, because two-way SMS is not supported in Egypt ([08](08-pestle-analysis.md), Technological). Replies update the appointment status. A cancellation triggers offers to waitlisted patients in order, and the first to accept takes the slot. Each week the owner receives a no-show report on WhatsApp. Consent and opt-out are recorded per patient. It matters because reminders raise attendance (attendance 67.8% without vs 78.6% with text reminders, [10](10-efas.md), O2) and the waitlist converts late cancellations into kept visits. |
| Target Audience / Users | Define the primary users or customer segments. Consider demographics (B2B vs B2C, company size, role), firmographics (industry, revenue, location), behavior (early adopters, existing customers). Use personas and the JTBD (Jobs to be Done) framework. | B2B: private clinics with 1 to 5 doctors in dentistry, dermatology, and pediatrics in Cairo and Giza governorates. Buyer: the clinic owner, usually a doctor. Daily user: the receptionist. Message recipient (B2C): the patient, or the guardian for pediatric patients. Early adopters: owner-doctors who already remind patients from a personal WhatsApp. JTBD: "When a patient books, I want them to come or free the slot early, so my schedule stays full without phone calls." Personas in [04-value-proposition-canvas.md](04-value-proposition-canvas.md) and [05-empathy-map.md](05-empathy-map.md). |
| Unique Value Proposition (UVP) | What makes this concept better or different from alternatives: the problem solved, why it is better or different, and the core benefits to the customer. | Fewer empty chairs without reminder calls: WhatsApp-first reminders with one-reply confirm or cancel, automatic SMS fallback, automatic waitlist refill of cancelled slots, and a weekly no-show report pushed to the owner, built Arabic-first for small Egyptian clinics, priced in EGP, and designed around the Personal Data Protection Law. Among the five Egyptian competitors compared in [06-market-comparison.md](06-market-comparison.md), none offers waitlist auto-fill or automatic SMS fallback. |
| Key Features | List of core features or components that deliver the solution. | (1) Clinic account with owner and receptionist logins; (2) appointment entry and CSV import; (3) scheduled WhatsApp reminders from approved Arabic and English utility templates; (4) one-reply confirm or cancel; (5) automatic SMS fallback with a confirm or cancel link; (6) waitlist offers for cancelled slots, first to accept wins; (7) weekly no-show report to the owner on WhatsApp; (8) per-patient consent and opt-out ledger, with guardian consent for children. Priorities in [14-moscow-method.md](14-moscow-method.md). |
| Business Model | Brief explanation of how revenue is generated (subscription, pay-per-use, B2B licensing, and so on). | B2B SaaS: a monthly subscription per clinic, billed in EGP and tiered by number of doctors, with a message allowance. Revenue streams in [03-lean-canvas.md](03-lean-canvas.md); price points in [21-roadmap-project-plan.md](21-roadmap-project-plan.md). |
| Expected Benefits / Impact | Quantify or describe the impact (cost savings, faster onboarding, reduced churn). | For clinics: fewer no-shows (published effect: no-shows 21% vs 15% with digital reminders, a 25% relative cut, [10](10-efas.md), O2), cancelled slots refilled from the waitlist, and fewer reminder calls for reception. For patients: fewer missed visits and earlier slots. The clinic-level effect will be measured against each pilot clinic's own four-week baseline. |
| Success Metrics | How success will be measured. | (1) Relative no-show reduction per clinic against its baseline; (2) reminder delivery rate across WhatsApp and SMS; (3) patient reply rate (confirm or cancel); (4) share of cancelled slots refilled from the waitlist; (5) paying clinics and monthly recurring revenue (EGP); (6) monthly logo churn; (7) owners' weekly-report read rate. Targets in [15-okrs.md](15-okrs.md). |
| Technology / Tools Used | Critical technologies, platforms, or APIs involved (frontend, backend, hosting, third-party services). | WhatsApp Business Platform through a direct Cloud API integration (utility templates, quick-reply buttons, status webhooks); an NTRA-licensed Egyptian SMS aggregator with a registered sender ID; a web dashboard and backend on the team's default stack (Angular with PrimeNG, Java 21 with Spring Boot, PostgreSQL), final choices in the SDD; hosting in Egypt or abroad pending the PDPL decision ([08](08-pestle-analysis.md), Legal); a local payment gateway for EGP billing. |
| Competitor Landscape | Reference the Market Comparison framework (file 06). | See [06-market-comparison.md](06-market-comparison.md). |
