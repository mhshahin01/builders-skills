# Product Lifecycle

The stages a product goes through from development and introduction to withdrawal from the market. It helps manage strategy, investment, marketing, and innovation over time. The stage focus is reference guidance; capture the product-specific plan and expected revenue posture per stage.

| Stage | Focus (reference) | Your product's activities & notes | Expected revenue posture |
|---|---|---|---|
| Development | Conceptualizing, building, and testing the product. Validate real user needs and identify MVP scope that delivers clear value to all personas. | 2026-10-01 to 2027-01-31: build the Must-have scope (41.5 developer-weeks estimated, [13-rice-framework.md](13-rice-framework.md)); Meta verification and utility templates; SMS sender ID; PDPC licence and DPO; recruit 15 pilot clinics and record their no-show baselines. | None; spend comes from the EGP 2,000,000 first-year budget. |
| Introduction | Launching the product to the market; gain early traction and feedback through marketing, awareness, and onboarding early adopters. | 2027-02 to 2027-09: pilot (February and March), paid launch in Cairo and Giza (April), referral and field sales, toward 40 paying clinics by 2027-09-30 ([15-okrs.md](15-okrs.md)). | Low and below costs: monthly recurring revenue up to about EGP 26,000 by 2027-09-30. |
| Growth | Rapid adoption and increasing market share; capitalize on momentum by scaling infrastructure, improving features, and expanding channels. | From 2027-10, after the seed: Should-have features, a sales team, then the next governorate and possibly other markets ([17-ansoff-matrix.md](17-ansoff-matrix.md)); the category grows 12.9% a year ([07-market-sizing-analysis.md](07-market-sizing-analysis.md)). | Rising toward the SOM of EGP 0.90M a year (about 116 clinics) by 2029-09-30. |
| Maturity | Maximizing profit and maintaining dominance; defend market position and extend lifecycle via optimization, differentiation, and retention. | Defend with the no-show dataset, clinic-software integrations, and annual plans; cut messaging cost per appointment. | Stable margin; growth from upsell more than from new clinics. |
| Decline | Managing reduced demand; decide whether to phase out, replace, or reinvent the product (cost-cutting, retirement, or pivoting). | Triggers: Meta repricing, rivals adding waitlist refill, or a booking platform giving reminders away. Options: pivot into a clinic CRM, license the engine to clinic-software vendors, or exit. | Declining; cut costs and consider a sale or licensing. |
